Lecture 14 · Adversarial generation and diffusion

Learn a distribution by competition—or by transporting noise

GANs learn a generator through a moving opponent. Rectified flows learn local velocities and integrate them into a sample. Both connect a simple random source to complex observations, but their objectives, diagnostics, and inference costs differ.

Two networks, two optimization directions 3:04

Our inspection project now needs plausible synthetic surface images: a diversity of normal finishes and defects under controlled conditions. Chapter13 explained how to specify a likelihood. A generative adversarial network, or GAN, takes another route. Draw a latent vector \(z\) from a known prior \(p_z\), then compute \(x=G_\theta(z)\). This deterministic generator induces a distribution \(p_G\) through the randomness of its input. Its parameters \(\theta\) determine which images receive probability mass, although the architecture need not provide a tractable density evaluator.

A discriminator \(D_\psi(x)\) estimates whether an image came from real training data rather than the generator. The parameter sets \(\theta\) and \(\psi\) are separate. With equally weighted real and generated populations, the original objective is \[ \min_\theta\max_\psi V(\theta,\psi),\qquad V=\mathbb E_{x\sim p_{\mathrm{data}}}\log D_\psi(x) +\mathbb E_{z\sim p_z}\log[1-D_\psi(G_\theta(z))]. \] For fixed generator, the discriminator increases \(V\) by assigning real examples larger probabilities and generated examples smaller probabilities. For fixed discriminator, the generator decreases the second term by making its samples receive larger real probabilities. The first term has no generator dependence.

Alternating updates therefore use opposite signs: \(\psi\leftarrow\psi+\eta_D\nabla_\psi V\) and \(\theta\leftarrow\theta-\eta_G\nabla_\theta V\), with positive learning rates \(\eta_D,\eta_G\). During the discriminator update, generated images can be detached from the generator graph. During the generator update, the discriminator's parameters remain fixed but differentiation must still pass through its input to reach the generator. Freezing weights and disabling all gradient computation are not the same operation.

The original GAN paper frames this as a game. Consequently a single value of \(V\) is not a reliable progress meter for image quality. A weak discriminator can be fooled by poor images; a stronger discriminator can make the same generator look worse. The individual losses remain useful diagnostics, but interpreting them requires the opponent's state and independent sample evaluation.

Repair weak generator gradients without confusing objectives 16:00

Suppose the discriminator's fake-image logit is \(a\), so \(D=\sigma(a)=1/(1+e^{-a})\). The minimax generator term is \(\ell_{\mathrm{sat}}=\log(1-\sigma(a))\). Its derivative with respect to the logit is \(-\sigma(a)\). When generated images are easily rejected, \(a\) is strongly negative and this derivative is close to zero. The weak signal then propagates through the discriminator and generator.

The common non-saturating alternative minimizes \(\ell_{\mathrm{ns}}=-\log\sigma(a)\), whose derivative is \(\sigma(a)-1\). At \(D=0.01\), the two logit derivatives are \(-0.01\) and \(-0.99\). Both updates encourage a larger real-image logit, but their magnitudes differ by a factor of99. The functions are not algebraically equivalent. Once this alternative is used, generator and discriminator optimize different scalar objectives rather than opposite directions of exactly the same expression.

The distinction between a derivative with respect to \(D\) and one with respect to \(a\) is essential. The derivative of \(\log(1-D)\) with respect to \(D\) is \(-1/(1-D)\), which is not tiny near zero. Saturation arises after multiplying by the sigmoid derivative. A correct explanation must follow the computational graph far enough to identify where the signal shrinks.

In the factory case, sharp synthetic scratches could fool a texture-sensitive discriminator while their geometry remains physically implausible. Another failure is mode collapse: many latent inputs produce only a few styles of defect. This is distinct from numerical overflow or NaNs, even though both can occur during unsuccessful training. A gallery of attractive samples demonstrates neither distributional coverage nor novelty. Hold out real parts, check nearest neighbors for copying, and measure performance on rare defect subtypes.

The ideal equilibrium does not prove training convergence 21:58

For a fixed generator, let \(p=p_{\mathrm{data}}(x)\) and \(q=p_G(x)\) at one location. Maximizing \(p\log D+q\log(1-D)\) gives derivative \(p/D-q/(1-D)=0\), hence \[ D^*(x)=\frac{p_{\mathrm{data}}(x)}{p_{\mathrm{data}}(x)+p_G(x)} \] where the denominator is positive. The second derivative is nonpositive and strictly negative when either weighted term is present in the interior. On the shared support, matching distributions gives \(D^*=1/2\). Where both densities vanish, the discriminator is unconstrained.

Under the ideal density assumptions, substitution gives \(V(G,D^*)=-\log4+2D_{\mathrm{JS}}(p_{\mathrm{data}}\Vert p_G)\), where Jensen–Shannon divergence averages the two KL divergences to their mixture. This makes the optimum meaningful. It does not say a finite discriminator reaches its best response each iteration, nor that alternating gradient updates converge. The distribution can be uniquely optimal while many parameterizations produce it.

The lecture illustrates architectural progress through DCGAN and StyleGAN, and shows latent interpolation. Smooth changes in \(z\) can give smooth changes in \(G_\theta(z)\), but continuity alone does not guarantee a path remains in a region of typical probability or preserves semantic identity. The original StyleGAN modulation mechanism and later StyleGAN versions should not be treated as one unchanged architecture. The important course-level lesson is that architecture and regularization strongly affect the game in practice.

A vanilla GAN also lacks an explicit encoder from an observed image back to its latent. One may optimize a latent numerically, or add an inference network in another formulation, but that is additional machinery. At ordinary generation time only the generator is needed, giving a relatively direct sampling route. The next model family exchanges that simplicity for repeated evaluations of a learned local update.

Make the direction of time explicit 30:32

The lecture introduces diffusion through a sequence of increasingly corrupted observations, then specializes to rectified flow. We preserve its convention: \(t=0\) means data, \(t=1\) means noise. Let \(x\sim p_{\mathrm{data}}\) and \(z\sim\mathcal N(0,I)\) be independently sampled tensors of the same shape. Set \[ x_t=(1-t)x+tz,\qquad v=z-x. \] The target \(v\) is the derivative of this interpolating path with respect to increasing \(t\). It points from data toward noise. A network \(v_\theta(x_t,t)\) minimizes \(\mathbb E\|v_\theta(x_t,t)-(z-x)\|_2^2\), with a specified distribution for the training time \(t\).

During training we know both endpoints because we sampled them. During generation we know only the initial noise. The network is not asked to retrieve the exact training image paired with a particular noise draw. At the same intermediate \(x_t\), several endpoint pairs may be possible. Squared-error regression learns their conditional mean velocity: \[ v^*(u,t)=\mathbb E[Z-X\mid X_t=u]. \] This follows by expanding conditional squared error around its conditional mean; the cross term has zero expectation. The extra derivation makes precise the geometric ambiguity discussed briefly in the lecture.

The rectified-flow paper formulates learned transport with an ordinary differential equation. Straight interpolation paths used to construct training targets do not imply every learned sampling trajectory is a straight line. The network averages possible directions, and the direction can change as the current point changes. Terminology varies across the literature: this chapter follows the lecture's broad grouping while distinguishing deterministic flow integration from stochastic diffusion sampling.

Sampling is numerical integration, with a sign and an error budget 40:05

Choose a decreasing grid \(1=t_K\gt t_{K-1}\gt\cdots\gt t_0=0\), initialize \(u_K\sim\mathcal N(0,I)\), and use an explicit Euler step \[ u_{k-1}=u_k+(t_{k-1}-t_k)v_\theta(u_k,t_k). \] The time increment is negative, so this subtracts a positive step size times the data-to-noise velocity. Calling it “gradient descent” is only a visual analogy: the predicted velocity need not be the gradient of any scalar objective. Exactly \(K\) intervals require \(K\) Euler evaluations. Updating once again at zero would overshoot the intended interval.

An original one-dimensional example exposes the distinction between pairwise paths and population transport. Let \(X\sim\mathcal N(m,s^2)\) and \(Z\sim\mathcal N(0,1)\) independently. Then \(X_t\) has mean \((1-t)m\) and variance \(q_t=(1-t)^2s^2+t^2\). Joint Gaussian conditioning gives \[ v^*(u,t)=-m+\frac{t-(1-t)s^2}{q_t}[u-(1-t)m]. \] The coefficient changes with time. The exact ODE solution starting at noise value \(z_0\) is \(u(t)=(1-t)m+\sqrt{q_t}\,z_0\). Differentiating this expression recovers the velocity because \(q_t'/2=t-(1-t)s^2\). At zero, the result is \(m+sz_0\), which has the desired data distribution.

We can now evaluate a sampler without relying on pictures. With \(m=2,s=0.5,z_0=1.25\), its exact endpoint is2.625. A coarse Euler solver has discretization error even though its velocity field is exact. More steps can reduce that error; they do not repair a systematically wrong learned field. Separating model error from integration error prevents treating every poor sample as a training failure.

Conditioning and guidance change the vector field 45:11

To request a particular defect type, add a condition \(y\) to the network, giving \(v_\theta(x_t,t,y)\). The training data now supplies image–condition pairs. Classifier-free guidance additionally trains the same model with the condition omitted on some examples, using a recognizable null condition \(\varnothing\). The dropout probability is a chosen hyperparameter; the lecture's illustrative coin flip is not a universal50% prescription.

Let \(v_y=v_\theta(u,t,y)\) and \(v_\varnothing=v_\theta(u,t,\varnothing)\). In the lecture's convention, \[ v_{\mathrm{cfg}}=(1+w)v_y-wv_\varnothing =v_\varnothing+(1+w)(v_y-v_\varnothing). \] Here \(w=0\) recovers ordinary conditional sampling. Libraries that call the multiplier on \(v_y-v_\varnothing\) a guidance scale instead use \(s=1+w\). A numerical value is uninterpretable until its convention is stated. For \(v_y=(2,-1)\), \(v_\varnothing=(1,1)\), and \(w=2\), the guided prediction is \((4,-5)\). The Euler update still uses the negative time increment.

The primary classifier-free guidance paper studies trading diversity against fidelity through combined conditional and unconditional predictions. Guidance is not free likelihood improvement: it changes the field and generally the sampled distribution. Stronger guidance can exaggerate features, reduce variety, or introduce artifacts. Two predictions per step can approximately double core network work, although batching and implementation determine actual latency.

For inspection augmentation, “more visibly scratched” is not necessarily more useful. The generator may amplify a stereotyped mark until it no longer resembles the subtle defects that dominate real errors. Choose guidance using downstream validation and diversity criteria, not solely a preference for dramatic images.

Noise scheduling allocates learning effort 49:52

Uniformly sampled \(t\) gives equal measure to equal-width time intervals. Alternatives change which prediction problems receive more training weight. A logit-normal time can be constructed as \(r\sim\mathcal N(\mu_r,\sigma_r^2)\), \(t=\sigma(r)\). Suitable parameters concentrate samples away from endpoints, but not every logit-normal parameter choice emphasizes the middle. Shifted schedules can emphasize noisier regions. These are objective-weighting choices unless importance weights compensate for the sampling change.

At \(t=1\), independence gives \(v^*(u,1)=u-\mathbb E[X]\); at \(t=0\), \(v^*(u,0)=\mathbb E[Z]-u=-u\) for centered Gaussian noise. The source's verbal shorthand about “predicting the mean” refers to the unknown endpoint. The velocity itself also includes the current point and its sign. Our Gaussian fixture checks these endpoint identities explicitly.

The primary Scaling Rectified Flow Transformers study examines design choices for high-resolution synthesis, including training-time weighting. The lecture motivates resolution dependence through correlations between neighboring pixels. In our setting, repeatedly photographing the same smooth metal surface does not create independent pixel evidence: many pixels can carry redundant information about illumination. A noise level that destroys a low-resolution structure may leave recoverable structure at another resolution.

Generate in a compressed space, then decode 52:31

A latent diffusion pipeline first learns an encoder \(E\) and decoder \(R\). An image of shape \(H\times W\times3\) becomes a latent of shape \((H/d)\times(W/d)\times c\), where \(d\) is a spatial downsampling factor and \(c\) is a latent channel count. The generative model then learns the distribution of encoded images while the encoder is frozen. At inference, it samples a latent by iterative updates and decodes that latent once.

For an illustrative \(256\times256\) image with \(d=8,c=16\), the latent is \(32\times32\times16\). Its16,384 scalar entries are twelve times fewer than the image's196,608 components. This is a scalar-count comparison, not a promised twelvefold runtime speedup. The model's channel widths, attention pattern, and decoder cost also matter.

The latent-diffusion paper combines a learned compressed representation with diffusion and conditioning. Autoencoder training can include perceptual and adversarial terms alongside a latent regularizer to improve reconstruction quality. Such hybrids explain why the lecture's earlier model families return in one system. They do not establish that every latent generator must use exactly the same VAE–GAN recipe.

Compression places a hard practical limit on our inspection application. If the autoencoder erases a hairline crack in its reconstruction, a downstream latent model cannot reliably preserve that crack merely by taking more denoising steps. Evaluate the reconstruction stage separately, especially on the smallest relevant defects, before measuring the generative stage. A high average image-quality score can conceal failure on tiny but operationally important features.

Count latent tokens before extrapolating image systems to video 55:52

A diffusion Transformer receives noisy latent tokens, a time embedding, and optional conditioning tokens. Time can modulate normalized activations through learned scale and shift. Text can enter through cross-attention or joint attention. Cross-attention lets image queries read text keys and values; joint attention places both types of tokens in a shared attention computation with the model's specified masking. These are architectural choices, not different definitions of the flow objective. The primary DiT paper establishes Transformer-based diffusion backbones.

Token arithmetic deserves an explicit correction. Starting from a \(1024\times1024\) image, an eightfold spatial reduction gives \(128\times128\) latent positions. Grouping them into \(2\times2\) patches gives \(64\times64=4096\) image tokens, not1024. The latter count would correspond to \(32\times32\) positions, such as a512-pixel image with the same reductions. This corrects an arithmetic inconsistency in the lecture's illustrative slide; it is not a claim about every configuration of a named model.

Video adds a temporal latent dimension. For a concrete shape \(33\times128\times72\times16\), patchifying into \(1\times2\times2\) groups yields \(33\cdot64\cdot36=76{,}032\) tokens. Dense attention scores scale quadratically in sequence length: compared with4096 tokens, this is approximately344.6 times as many score entries. Kernel optimizations can reduce materialized memory, but do not by themselves remove all pairwise arithmetic.

The lecture surveys text-to-video systems and a release announced that day. Those examples locate the material in May2025; they are not a current ranking. For a factory video generator, temporal coherence must include conservation of part identity, camera motion, and plausible mechanics. A sequence that looks credible frame by frame may still invent or erase a scratch between frames. Text alignment and visual quality therefore need temporal and task-specific checks.

Several mathematical views share components without being identical 1:02:30

Repeated model evaluations motivate distillation: train a student to approximate a teacher's multi-step sampling behavior with fewer steps. The lecture mentions this family without deriving a particular algorithm. Reducing the number of steps of an unchanged Euler solver is not itself distillation. A trained student can embody a different update rule, and its quality–speed tradeoff must be measured rather than assumed.

A general corruption can be written \(x_t=a(t)x+b(t)z\), with a target \(c(t)x+d(t)z\). The functions determine both the noise process and the prediction parameterization. For independent unit-variance coordinates, \(\operatorname{Var}(x_t)=a(t)^2+b(t)^2\); variance preservation requires this sum to equal one. The rectified-flow choice \(a=1-t,b=t\) instead has variance1/2 at the midpoint when both endpoints have unit variance. A variance-exploding construction can keep \(a=1\) while increasing \(b\).

A score is \(s_t(u)=\nabla_u\log p_t(u)\), a derivative with respect to the sample, not the network parameters. For a Gaussian \(\mathcal N(m_t,q_t)\), \(s_t(u)=-(u-m_t)/q_t\). The primary score-based SDE paper connects noising processes, reverse-time stochastic dynamics, and a corresponding probability-flow ODE. A neural network estimates a field used by a numerical solver; it is not itself the solver. Our deterministic Euler example integrates an ODE and contains no Brownian increment.

Finally, autoregression can return in a compressed discrete space. A codebook replaces each latent position with an integer index; an autoregressive model predicts those indices and a decoder reconstructs pixels. Neural Discrete Representation Learning provides a primary entry point. Continuous latents support continuous noising; discrete tokens support categorical prediction. Both depend on the fidelity of their representation stage. This concludes the lecture's unifying idea: practical generative systems combine components whose roles must be understood separately.

An exact velocity field makes sampler error measurable

The original experiment below checks both GAN logit derivatives and Gaussian transport. It uses no trained image model. Its exact solution provides an independent target for Euler integration.

import numpy as np
from numerical_lab import central_gradient

a = np.array([np.log(0.01/0.99)])
sigmoid = lambda q: 1/(1+np.exp(-q))
sat = central_gradient(lambda q: np.log(1-sigmoid(q[0])), a)
nonsat = central_gradient(lambda q: -np.log(sigmoid(q[0])), a)
assert np.allclose(sat, [-0.01], atol=1e-8)
assert np.allclose(nonsat, [-0.99], atol=1e-8)

m, s, noise = 2.0, 0.5, 1.25
def velocity(u, t):
    q = (1-t)**2*s*s + t*t
    return -m + (t-(1-t)*s*s)*(u-(1-t)*m)/q

def sample(steps):
    u = noise
    for k in range(steps, 0, -1):
        t = k/steps
        u -= velocity(u, t)/steps
    return u

exact = m+s*noise
errors = [abs(sample(k)-exact) for k in (20, 200, 2000)]
assert errors[2] < errors[1] < errors[0]
assert errors[2] < 0.002
assert np.isclose(velocity(noise, 1), noise-m)
assert np.isclose(velocity(3.0, 0), -3.0)
vy, vnull = np.array([2., -1.]), np.array([1., 1.])
assert np.allclose(3*vy-2*vnull, [4., -5.])
assert (1024//8//2)**2 == 4096
assert 33*(128//2)*(72//2) == 76032
print("Euler endpoint errors at20,200,2000steps:", errors)

Six exercises with complete solutions

1. Can a discriminator value of one-half certify success?

No. At ideal equilibrium it is the optimal response to matching distributions, but an untrained constant discriminator also returns one-half for arbitrary generated images. The conclusion requires a sufficiently expressive discriminator at its optimum, not merely its numerical output. Independent coverage and quality evaluation remain necessary.

2. Compute an optimal discriminator on two outcomes.

Let real probabilities be \((.8,.2)\), generated probabilities \((.4,.6)\), with equal source priors. The optimal real probabilities are \(.8/(.8+.4)=2/3\) and \(.2/(.2+.6)=1/4\). A generated sample at the second outcome is easier to identify because the generator overproduces it. These values follow from the local derivative calculation, not from image semantics.

3. Why can independent straight training paths yield curved trajectories?

The network sees only \(X_t=u,t\), so under squared loss its optimum is the conditional average \(\mathbb E[Z-X\mid X_t=u]\). Different endpoint pairs can pass through the same intermediate region with different directions. The conditional population changes with \(u,t\), so the average direction varies along a sampled path. Straightness of each supervision pair is insufficient to establish straightness of this averaged-field trajectory.

4. Correct the guidance convention and step sign.

For \(w=2\), the lecture uses \(3v_y-2v_\varnothing\). This equals \(v_\varnothing+s(v_y-v_\varnothing)\) at \(s=3\). With our vectors the result is \((4,-5)\). Starting from \((1,1)\) with time increment \(-0.1\), Euler gives \((1,1)-.1(4,-5)=(.6,1.5)\). Using \(+.1\) reverses the intended time direction.

5. Explain the variance dip of linear interpolation.

For independent centered \(X,Z\) of unit variance, covariance is zero, so the interpolation variance is \((1-t)^2+t^2\). At \(t=.5\) it is.5. Unit coefficients summing to one preserve a convex combination of means, not the variance. A variance-preserving schedule instead needs squared coefficients summing to one.

6. Where is the bottleneck if generated cracks disappear?

First reconstruct held-out cracked images through the encoder and decoder alone. If cracks disappear there, representation fidelity is a bottleneck before generation begins. If reconstructions preserve them but generated samples omit them, inspect training coverage, conditioning, guidance, and the latent generative model. If static frames preserve cracks but video loses them, add temporal consistency checks. This staged test identifies different failure boundaries rather than changing every component simultaneously.
