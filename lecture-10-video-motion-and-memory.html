Chapter 10 · CS231N, Spring 2025

Video understanding: motion, memory, and selective observation

A video is not merely a larger image. Time adds order, motion, sampling choices, and opportunities to combine sight with sound. A useful model must preserve the temporal evidence needed by its task.

Based on the complete guest lecture by Ruohan Gao and all 103 pages of official slide text. The sampling, temporal-order, optical-flow, filter-inflation, and cost examples are original. Historical benchmark charts are treated as contextual evidence, not current model rankings.

1. Time changes both the data and the question 3:05

Our inspection camera now observes a robot placing a part into a fixture. A photograph can show the part and gripper, but it may not distinguish successful insertion from withdrawal. The same visible configurations can appear in different temporal orders. We need to decide whether the action completed, when a slip occurred, and perhaps which sound accompanied contact.

Represent an RGB clip as \(X\in\mathbb R^{C\times T\times H\times W}\), with \(C=3\), \(T\) frames, and spatial dimensions \(H,W\). A batch adds a leading dimension. The channel axis is distinct from the three axes over which a spatiotemporal convolution slides. Saying “3D convolution” refers to time, height, and width, not to the RGB channels.

Raw storage grows quickly. At 30 frames per second, one minute of \(1920\times1080\) RGB video stored as one byte per channel requires \(60\cdot30\cdot1920\cdot1080\cdot3=11{,}197{,}440{,}000\) bytes, about 11.2 decimal GB. Compression can reduce file size substantially, but decoded tensors and intermediate network activations still consume memory. Float32 input alone uses four times the byte storage of this unsigned-byte representation.

The lecture therefore begins with short clips at reduced frame rate and spatial resolution. A 16-frame \(112\times112\) RGB clip needs \(602{,}112\) bytes in uint8, exactly 588 KiB. At five samples per second, 16 frame slots correspond to a nominal 3.2-second acquisition window; the timestamp difference between the first and last sample is \(15/5=3\) seconds. Stating the convention avoids an off-by-one confusion when aligning labels.

Clip classification returns a label for a sampled interval. Whole-video classification aggregates evidence across intervals. Temporal localization predicts action intervals. Spatiotemporal detection additionally localizes actors in image coordinates. These tasks can use related feature extractors, but they require different targets and should not be evaluated as interchangeable forms of “video understanding.”

2. Sampling is part of the model 6:44

A large video cannot always be processed frame by frame. Uniform sampling is simple and reproducible, while random sampling can provide varied training clips. The critical question is whether the sampled observations preserve the event that determines the label. No downstream model can recover a brief event that leaves no evidence in the selected frames or other inputs.

As an original idealized calculation, suppose an event occupies a fraction \(r\) of a video and we draw \(m\) independent uniformly random frame times. The probability of missing it every time is \((1-r)^m\), so the chance of observing it at least once is \(1-(1-r)^m\). A two-second event in a 100-second video has \(r=0.02\). Ten draws give only \(1-0.98^{10}\approx0.183\) probability of a hit. To reach at least 95% under these assumptions requires

\[ m\ge\frac{\log(0.05)}{\log(0.98)}, \]

hence 149 draws after rounding upward. Real clips cover intervals rather than isolated times, samples may be correlated, and events can have residual visual effects, so this is a transparent teaching model rather than a universal operational estimate.

Fixed-rate sampling also introduces aliasing. If a marker oscillates as \(z(t)=\cos(2\pi ft)\) and is sampled at integer multiples of \(1/f_s\), different frequencies can produce identical sampled values. For example, a five-cycle-per-second motion sampled at five samples per second appears constant at those sample times. Increasing model size cannot disambiguate identical observations without additional assumptions or measurements.

Training and test splitting should respect the video’s origin. Neighboring clips from the same recording share backgrounds, actors, camera properties, and sometimes almost identical frames. Randomly placing them in different splits can make evaluation measure recognition of recording-specific cues. For the factory case, split by production run, physical part, or acquisition session according to the intended generalization question.

3. Establish what a frame model can and cannot do 8:45

The lecture recommends a single-frame classifier as a strong baseline. Many action datasets contain informative scenery and objects: a swimming pool or tennis racket may predict a label without detailed motion analysis. This can be useful signal, but a good benchmark score does not establish that the model recognizes temporal order.

If frame probabilities are \(p_t(c)\), averaging gives \(\bar p(c)=T^{-1}\sum_t p_t(c)\). This is a mixture of per-frame predictions. Averaging logits before softmax is different because softmax is nonlinear. Multiplying probabilities and renormalizing represents another rule, closer to combining evidence under stronger conditional assumptions. None should be substituted silently for another.

Late fusion first extracts frame features \(z_t\in\mathbb R^D\). Concatenation produces a \(TD\)-dimensional representation and can preserve order, but an MLP with hidden width \(M\) then needs \(TDM\) weights in its first layer. It also generally assumes a fixed number of input positions. Mean pooling instead gives \(\bar z=T^{-1}\sum_tz_t\), keeping width \(D\) and accepting variable numbers of frames.

Pooling has an exact limitation: \(\bar z\) is invariant to any permutation of frames. Consider scalar features \((0,1,2)\) for insertion progress and \((2,1,0)\) for withdrawal. Both average to one. Any deterministic classifier that receives only that mean must output the same result for both sequences. No amount of training can overcome information that the representation has deliberately erased.

Early fusion stacks frames into channels, reshaping \(C\times T\times H\times W\) into \(CT\times H\times W\). Its first 2D convolution mixes all frame positions at once. This can detect order-specific patterns, but weights tied to an early frame position are not automatically reused when the same motion occurs later in the clip. Early and late fusion are useful baselines with explicit inductive biases, not simply inferior versions of every 3D model.

4. Share motion detectors over time 18:23

A 3D convolution mixes local neighborhoods in both space and time. For one output channel \(o\), a stride-one cross-correlation can be written

\[ Y_{o,t,i,j}=b_o+\sum_c\sum_{\tau=0}^{k_t-1} \sum_{u=0}^{k_h-1}\sum_{v=0}^{k_w-1} W_{o,c,\tau,u,v}X_{c,t+\tau,i+u,j+v}. \]

Padding and strides modify the sampled indices and valid output range. The kernel spans all input channels but only a local temporal window \(k_t\). The same temporal pattern can therefore be recognized wherever the kernel is applied. Ignoring boundaries and using stride one, the operation is shift equivariant: shifting the input shifts the response. Pooling or an appropriate readout can then make an output more invariant. Equivariance of the feature map and invariance of the final decision are different properties.

A temporal difference filter \((-1,1)\) gives responses \(x_{t+1}-x_t\). On \((0,1,2)\) it returns \((1,1)\); on the reversed sequence it returns \((-1,-1)\). Unlike mean pooling, this local operator distinguishes increasing from decreasing evidence. A learned kernel can generalize this idea to motion patterns across multiple channels and spatial positions.

Ignoring biases, a full 3D convolution has \(C_{\mathrm{out}}C_{\mathrm{in}}k_tk_hk_w\) weights. With input channels three, output channels eight, and a \(3\times3\times3\) kernel, that is 648 weights. A spatial-only \(3\times3\) convolution with the same channels has 216. Applying either kernel across many output positions multiplies arithmetic cost without multiplying its shared parameter count.

Temporal receptive fields grow by the same recurrence used for spatial receptive fields. Let \(r\) be receptive-field width in input frames and \(j\) the spacing between neighboring outputs. A layer of kernel width \(k\) and stride \(s\) updates \(r'=r+(k-1)j\), \(j'=sj\), starting from \(r=j=1\). A width-three convolution, width-two stride-two pool, and another width-three convolution give \((r,j)=(3,1),(4,2),(8,2)\). The resulting output can depend on eight input frames, before any global pooling.

C3D made small spatiotemporal kernels a practical learned feature extractor. Its historical role includes reusable pretrained representations, which let researchers train lightweight downstream classifiers without retraining the entire video network. The lecture’s arithmetic charts illustrate cost; comparing models fairly also requires identical clip lengths, resolutions, test views, and operation-count conventions.

The Sports-1M study supplies the lecture’s early comparison of single-frame, early-fusion, late-fusion, and spatiotemporal models. Its modest temporal gains motivate testing what a benchmark actually requires. UCF-101 and Kinetics provide other action-recognition settings; their different category sets, collection processes, and evaluation protocols prevent direct comparison of percentages across datasets.

Dataset availability can also change when examples are distributed as video URLs. A deleted or edited recording changes the recoverable dataset even if its identifier remains in a manifest. Reproducible experiments should record which examples were actually obtained, their time intervals, preprocessing, and split membership. Missing examples can change class balance or difficulty, so silently training on a surviving subset is not an exact reproduction. In a local inspection project, immutable recording identifiers and explicit clip timestamps serve the same purpose without requiring a public benchmark.

5. Optical flow supplies an explicit motion representation 40:04

Appearance and motion can be modeled separately. Optical flow estimates a two-component image displacement field \(F_t(x,y)=(u,v)\) between adjacent frames. Under brightness constancy, the same visible point approximately satisfies \(I_{t+1}(x+u,y+v)=I_t(x,y)\). This is a modeling assumption about image intensity, not a law of physical motion.

For small displacements, first-order expansion gives the optical-flow constraint

\[ I_xu+I_yv+I_t\approx0, \]

where \(I_x,I_y\) are spatial intensity derivatives and \(I_t\) is temporal change over the chosen interval. A single scalar equation cannot uniquely determine two motion components. Along a straight edge, motion tangent to the edge can be invisible locally: this is the aperture problem. Neighborhood regularity, multiple observations, learned priors, or other constraints are needed.

For an original two-observation fixture, gradients \((I_x,I_y)=(1,0)\) with \(I_t=-2\), and \((0,1)\) with \(I_t=1\), imply \(u=2,v=-1\). If both spatial gradients point horizontally, the vertical component remains undetermined. The rank of the local gradient system exposes the ambiguity directly.

Flow describes apparent image motion. Camera motion can move every background pixel without an object acting, while lighting changes, specularities, occlusions, and motion blur violate simple brightness assumptions. Displacement units must also be stated: pixels per frame interval are not the same as physical velocity or pixels per second. Changing frame rate changes the relationship.

Two-stream networks process appearance and stacked flow through separate branches. A sequence of \(T\) frames yields \(T-1\) adjacent flow fields, each with two channels, giving a \(2(T-1)\times H\times W\) stack. The two prediction streams are fused. This provides a useful motion prior, but computing flow adds cost and can propagate estimation errors.

The lecture observes strong motion-only results on historical action benchmarks and suggests reduced appearance overfitting as one explanation. That is a hypothesis, not a universal guarantee. A motion stream can also learn shortcuts from camera shake or editing patterns. In our inspection example, a fixed camera and controlled lighting simplify flow, while a moving wrist camera makes camera/object motion separation much harder.

6. Local clips and long events require different memory 45:11

A short clip can reveal a slip, but deciding whether a part was inserted and later removed may require relating events separated by minutes. One approach feeds clip features into an RNN or LSTM. A many-to-one readout predicts a whole-video label; a many-to-many readout predicts a label at each time. Freezing the feature extractor reduces training memory but prevents it from adapting to information the temporal task needs.

A convolutional recurrent model preserves spatial feature maps instead of flattening them. With hidden map \(H_t\) and current input map \(X_t\), a simple update is \(H_t=\tanh(W_h*H_{t-1}+W_x*X_t+b)\), where \(*\) denotes a spatial convolution. Both terms must have matching output shapes. More elaborate gated units replace affine products with convolutions in the same spirit.

The resulting state can depend structurally on all previous inputs, but that does not mean it preserves arbitrary historical detail. Finite state, forgetting, gradient propagation, and training distribution remain the limitations derived in Chapter 7. Likewise, “infinite temporal extent” in an architecture diagram means no fixed finite receptive-field cutoff in the recurrence, not perfect memory of an infinite video.

For online inspection, causality is another constraint. A system deciding whether to halt the conveyor now cannot use a future frame. An offline bidirectional model can use both earlier and later evidence. Compare them only under the intended observation rules. A high offline score may rely on seeing the outcome of the very event an online system is supposed to anticipate.

7. Global communication can combine distant space-time positions 51:43

A non-local block applies attention-like communication over a video feature volume. Pointwise \(1\times1\times1\) projections produce query, key, and value features. Flattening \(T,H,W\) gives \(N=THW\) positions. Weighted interactions produce a new feature at each position; an output projection restores channel width before a residual addition.

The residual operation adds tensors of matching shape. It is not channel concatenation, which would change width and require a different following operator. Because the block communicates across both space and time, a feature on the gripper can read information from a distant contact region. That access is potentially valuable but does not establish that the learned model uses it faithfully.

The quadratic cost is severe. With 16 time steps and a \(14\times14\) spatial feature grid, \(N=3136\), so one dense attention matrix has \(9{,}834{,}496\) entries. At four bytes each, it occupies about 37.5 MiB before other activations or gradients. Applying global attention directly to full-resolution video pixels would be much more expensive.

Factorized space-time attention separates spatial and temporal interaction. With \(S\) spatial tokens per frame, full attention has \(T^2S^2\) pairwise entries, while one spatial stage plus one temporal stage has \(TS^2+ST^2\). For \(T=16,S=196\), these counts are \(9{,}834{,}496\) and \(664{,}832\), about a 14.8-fold difference in pair counts. The factorized computation is a different composition, not an algebraically identical replacement for unrestricted attention.

The lecture also points to multiscale token reduction and VideoMAE as ways to improve representation learning and resource use. Masked reconstruction exploits redundancy by learning from partially observed video tokens. Its training task and mask design matter; a benchmark improvement should not be attributed to architecture alone when pretraining data and evaluation protocols also change.

8. Reuse an image model with a checkable initialization 54:26

I3D inflates image-network operations into temporal ones. For a 2D kernel \(W\), a simple initialization creates \(k_t\) temporal slices \(W_\tau=W/k_t\). On a constant video \(X_t=X\), each valid temporal window produces

\[ \sum_{\tau=1}^{k_t}(W/k_t)*X=W*X. \]

This exact identity explains the division. Copying the kernel without scaling would multiply the convolution response by \(k_t\). Bias is added once after the sum. The claim holds where every temporal slice sees the same image; zero padding at clip boundaries can violate that condition and change boundary outputs.

For a scalar spatial operation \(W=3\), constant input two, and three temporal slices, each inflated weight is one, giving \(1\cdot2+1\cdot2+1\cdot2=6\), matching the original \(3\cdot2\). Without division, the output is 18. This initialization preserves a known behavior while allowing subsequent video training to specialize different temporal slices.

Architecture transfer and weight transfer are separate ideas. Replacing 2D kernels by 3D kernels transfers a structural design; copying and scaling trained weights transfers a particular parameter setting. Neither proves improved performance on the factory’s videos. Domain shift in illumination, viewpoints, motion, and action labels must still be assessed.

9. Locate events and inspect what the model learned 59:36

Video models can be inspected by optimizing inputs to increase a class score, extending the visualization ideas from Chapter 9. A teaching objective might maximize \(s_c(X)-\lambda R(X)\), where \(R\) penalizes implausibly rough images or flow. Different regularizers reveal different preferred patterns. The result is a model-and-regularizer-dependent construction, not a recovered real event or a causal explanation.

Temporal action localization predicts an interval \([a,b]\) and label. The temporal intersection-over-union of intervals \([a,b]\) and \([c,d]\) is

\[ \frac{\max(0,\min(b,d)-\max(a,c))} {(b-a)+(d-c)-\max(0,\min(b,d)-\max(a,c))}. \]

For a target event \([10,14]\) seconds and prediction \([11,15]\), the intersection lasts three seconds and the union five, giving 0.6. A classifier can name the action correctly yet localize it too late to support an operational decision.

Spatiotemporal detection adds actor boxes or tracks over time. Multiple actors can perform different or simultaneous actions. A whole-clip label loses that assignment structure. In inspection, “slip occurred” is less informative than identifying which part slipped, at what time, and while which gripper was engaged. The output contract must carry those distinctions.

10. Sound can be evidence and a preview signal 62:54

The lecture closes by adding audio. A video file may contain sounds that reveal contact, speech, or activity hidden from the camera. Conversely, visual motion can help separate overlapping sound sources. VisualVoice studies audio-visual speech separation, while the lecture also discusses separating instruments using visual object and motion cues.

A simple mixture model is \(y(t)=\sum_{j=1}^{J}s_j(t)+\epsilon(t)\), where \(s_j\) are source signals and \(\epsilon\) is noise. One observed waveform generally does not uniquely identify all sources. Visual evidence supplies additional constraints: whose mouth is moving, which instrument is active, or which machine just contacted a part. Synchronization errors weaken those constraints, and an offscreen source may have no visual counterpart.

Audio spectrogram patches and visual patches can both enter a transformer, but their axes have different meanings. Audio time must align with video time; frequency is not an image coordinate. Missing audio, edited soundtracks, and sensor delays should be tested explicitly. A multimodal model can exploit misleading correlations just as a visual model can.

Listen to Look uses audio as a preview mechanism for efficient visual processing. More generally, a cheap selector can choose which clips deserve an expensive model. If a full visual pass costs \(c_v\) per clip over \(M\) clips, processing all costs \(Mc_v\). A selector costing \(c_s\) for every clip and retaining \(k\) clips costs \(Mc_s+kc_v\). Savings require \(c_s/c_v<1-k/M\), before considering decoding and transfer overhead.

Efficiency therefore trades against coverage. A selector trained to prioritize loud events can miss a silent crack. Report event recall and total pipeline cost together. Egocentric video adds moving viewpoints and social-interaction questions; language-connected video models add question answering and descriptions. Fluent output still needs grounding in sampled evidence. A system should distinguish “the observed frames show insertion” from “the complete video proves successful insertion” when intervening events were never examined.

11. Test the temporal assumptions directly

These fixtures verify the original arithmetic and counterexamples. They use small arrays to expose what sampling, pooling, and initialization preserve. They do not claim to reproduce the lecture’s benchmark results.

import math
import numpy as np

raw_bytes = 60 * 30 * 1920 * 1080 * 3
assert raw_bytes == 11197440000
assert 16 * 112 * 112 * 3 == 588 * 1024
hit = 1 - .98**10
assert .182 < hit < .184
minimum = math.ceil(math.log(.05) / math.log(.98))
assert minimum == 149
assert 1 - .98**minimum >= .95
assert 1 - .98**(minimum - 1) < .95

forward = np.array([0., 1., 2.])
reverse = forward[::-1]
assert forward.mean() == reverse.mean()
assert np.allclose(np.diff(forward), [1, 1])
assert np.allclose(np.diff(reverse), [-1, -1])
sample_times = np.arange(6) / 5
assert np.allclose(np.cos(2*np.pi*5*sample_times), 1)

r, jump = 1, 1
trajectory = []
for kernel, stride in [(3, 1), (2, 2), (3, 1)]:
    r += (kernel - 1) * jump
    jump *= stride
    trajectory.append((r, jump))
assert trajectory == [(3, 1), (4, 2), (8, 2)]

spatial_grad = np.array([[1., 0.], [0., 1.]])
temporal_grad = np.array([-2., 1.])
flow = np.linalg.solve(spatial_grad, -temporal_grad)
assert np.allclose(flow, [2, -1])
assert np.linalg.matrix_rank([[1, 0], [2, 0]]) == 1

kernel2d = np.array([[1., 2.], [-1., .5]])
image = np.array([[2., 1.], [0., 4.]])
kernel3d = np.repeat(kernel2d[None] / 3, 3, axis=0)
video = np.repeat(image[None], 3, axis=0)
assert np.isclose(np.sum(kernel3d * video), np.sum(kernel2d * image))
full = (16 * 196)**2
factorized = 16 * 196**2 + 196 * 16**2
assert full == 9834496 and factorized == 664832
assert np.isclose(3 / (4 + 4 - 3), .6)

12. Exercises with complete solutions

1. Separate file size from training-memory requirements.

A codec can compress repeated frames and spatial redundancy in a stored video. Training normally decodes samples into arrays and stores intermediate activations needed for backpropagation. Thus a small file can still create large tensors. The 588 KiB uint8 clip becomes 2352 KiB in float32 before any feature maps, model weights, gradients, or optimizer state are counted.

2. Prove that averaging cannot distinguish an action from its reverse in general.

For features \(z_1,\ldots,z_T\), reversing them changes the sum to \(\sum_tz_{T+1-t}\), which is the same sum under index substitution. Any head receiving only the average has identical input. A temporal difference or another order-sensitive feature can distinguish some such pairs, but only if sampling and the frame representation preserve the relevant changes.

3. Compute the temporal width of a two-stream flow input.

Eight RGB frames create seven adjacent frame pairs. Each flow field contains horizontal and vertical displacement, so stacking produces 14 channels. These are motion channels in a 2D network input, not 14 time steps of an RGB clip. If flow is measured over a two-frame gap instead, both the number and physical interpretation of those fields must be specified again.

4. Explain why a temporal edge detector generalizes across positions.

The kernel \((-1,1)\) computes the same difference at each adjacent pair. A rise from zero to one yields response one wherever it occurs in a valid window. A stride-one shift of the sequence shifts that response, apart from boundaries. This is equivariance. Taking the maximum positive response afterward can remove dependence on where that rise occurred, producing an invariant summary over the evaluated positions.

5. Find the boundary limitation of inflated filters.

The constant-video identity assumes all \(k_t\) slices see the same frame. With zero temporal padding, a boundary window includes zero slices, so its sum has fewer nonzero copies and generally has smaller response. Valid convolution, replicated boundary frames, or evaluating only interior positions can restore the stated condition. The bias remains a single post-sum addition.

6. Evaluate a cheap clip selector.

Let \(M=100,c_v=10,c_s=0.5,k=20\). Full processing costs 1000 units, while selection plus retained processing costs \(100(0.5)+20(10)=250\), a fourfold reduction under this simplified accounting. If the selector misses the only defective interval, the saved computation has destroyed task success. Measure selector recall, final accuracy, and actual end-to-end latency rather than reporting only retained clip count.

13. A video model includes its observation policy

Temporal kernels, recurrent states, attention, and audio fusion answer different information-flow questions. Their effectiveness depends on which evidence is sampled and preserved. For the inspection system, the most useful next experiment is a controlled set of order reversals, brief events, camera-motion changes, and missing-audio cases. The following chapter explains how to scale training while keeping the mathematical objective and its numerical implementation aligned.
