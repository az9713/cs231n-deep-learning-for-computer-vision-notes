A route through visual learning

This independent textbook follows all 18 recordings in the Spring 2025 course. Begin with linear algebra (vectors, matrix products, eigenvalues), multivariable calculus (chain rule, gradients), probability (conditioning, expectation, Bayes' rule), and basic Python/NumPy. The chapters define their own symbols and supply small checks; they do not assume that recalling a named architecture is the same as understanding it.

Chapters1–4 turn visual ambiguity into a learning objective and derive the gradients used to fit it. Chapters5–8 introduce spatial structure, practical training, recurrent memory, and attention. Chapters9–12 extend prediction to localized outputs, video, distributed computation, and self-supervised features. Chapters13–16 model distributions, generate images, represent3D scenes, and connect vision to language. Chapters17–18 put perception inside action and human decision systems.

An original manufactured-part inspection case connects most chapters: recognizing a defect becomes locating it, tracking it, describing it, reconstructing its shape, and deciding what to do. The final guest lectures use distinct towel-folding, visual grounding, and hospital-monitoring cases where those better expose their subject. These examples and their numerical values are teaching constructions, not Stanford benchmark results.

For a first reading, follow the chapter order and attempt each exercise before opening its solution. For implementation, run the interactive laboratory, then the executable examples embedded in the chapters. For research, follow the primary-paper links beside the relevant claims and use the source map to distinguish lecture material, research extensions, and original derivations. Timestamp links return to the lecture at each major source-grounded section.

The local reader includes bundled mathematical rendering, light/dark themes, adjustable text, chapter navigation, code copying, solution expansion, and print formatting. External links require a connection; reading and the interactive experiments work offline. Source recordings and raw machine transcripts are deliberately outside this repository. Machine transcription can contain errors, so technical names and important mechanisms were cross-checked against official slides and primary sources.
